\# CareFlow — Viva Preparation



\## Member 1 (Data)



\### Q1: How is the synthetic data generated and why?

\*\*Answer:\*\* The data is generated by a Python script (`src/data/generate\_events.py`). Because we cannot use real patient data (PHI), we simulate realistic Emergency Room workflows. I used a random seed (`42`) to make the generation 100% deterministic, so every time the pipeline runs, it produces the exact same 2,741 events across 500 patients.



\### Q2: What are the planted ground truth inefficiencies?

\*\*Answer:\*\* The project brief requires a "hidden bottleneck." We deliberately planted a \*\*40% loop-back rate\*\* where patients are sent from `X-Ray` back to `Triage` (simulating missing paperwork). We also planted standard variance: 55% of patients get an X-Ray, 45% get a Doctor Review, and 40% are admitted to the ward. This gives M2's process-mining algorithm something real to discover.



\### Q3: What does your validation rule catch?

\*\*Answer:\*\* I built a 10-point validation script (`src/data/validate\_events.py`) and a Pytest suite. It catches missing IDs, empty activities, invalid shift names, malformed timestamps (not `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS`), duplicate timestamps within a case, and out-of-order events. If any of these fail, the pipeline exits and prevents bad data from reaching the dashboard.



\### Q4: How did you prove the data is realistic?

\*\*Answer:\*\* I wrote `docs/data\_realism\_audit.md`. It proves we have zero PHI (Patient Health Information) because all names and IDs are fictional. It also proves structural realism: wait times are randomized between 10 and 45 minutes, and staff are rotated across Morning, Evening, and Night shifts to mimic a real 24-hour ER.



\### Q5: Explain the full pipeline architecture.

\*\*Answer:\*\* 

1\. \*\*My part:\*\* My generator creates `event\_log.csv` and my validator guarantees it passes 10/10 checks.

2\. \*\*Member 2's part:\*\* PM4Py analyzes the CSV, finds the 40% bottleneck, and exports tables to the `outputs/` folder.

3\. \*\*Member 3's part:\*\* The Streamlit dashboard reads the `outputs/` folder and visualizes the bottlenecks for the user.
